01 · Cheap Term Insurance

Protect the years your family depends on your income.

Term life insurance can provide a large death benefit for the years when your mortgage, children, spouse or business still depend on you.

The Real Question

If your paycheck disappeared tomorrow, how many years could your family keep the house, fund college, pay debt and maintain their lifestyle?

“Why do I need term life insurance?”
Your future earnings may be one of your family's largest financial assets. A properly sized term policy can create immediate liquidity if you die during the years when your family still depends on your income.
Ask us how much coverage may be appropriate →
“I already have life insurance through work.”
Employer coverage can be valuable. The issue is whether it is enough and whether you keep it if you leave your employer. Individual coverage can be designed around your own family's needs and is generally independent of your job.
“I'm healthy. Why buy it now?”
Life insurance pricing is heavily influenced by your age and health when you apply. Waiting can mean higher premiums or fewer underwriting options later.
“Should both spouses have coverage?”
Often yes. The death of either spouse can create major economic consequences, including lost income, childcare costs, household support, caregiving expenses and reduced financial flexibility.

02 · Passive Retirement Income

Turn part of your savings into income you cannot outlive.

Retirement assets have different jobs. Some money can pursue growth. Some should remain liquid. Some can be positioned to create dependable lifetime income.

The Retirement Question

How much of your monthly lifestyle would you like covered even if the market drops the year you retire?

“I already have a 401(k). Why would I need an annuity?”
A 401(k) is an account. It does not automatically create a lifetime paycheck. Retirement planning has two different jobs: accumulation and distribution. Certain annuities can help with the distribution job by creating contractually defined lifetime income from a portion of retirement assets.
“I invested in NVIDIA. Why would I move money away from growth?”
You may not need to move all of it. Growth assets and retirement-income assets solve different problems. Growth assets pursue upside. Income assets can help pay essential expenses without forcing you to sell stocks during an unfavorable market.
“What happens when the market crashes?”
Fixed indexed annuities generally protect the contract from direct negative index returns. Their upside can also be limited through participation rates, caps, spreads or other index-crediting rules. Withdrawals, rider charges and surrender provisions can still affect values.
“Should I put all my retirement money into an annuity?”
Usually that would be a poor starting assumption. A retirement plan should consider liquidity, growth, emergency reserves, taxes and income. The better question is how much of your assets should be assigned to your retirement-income floor.

03 · Long-Term Care

Protect your retirement plan from becoming your care plan.

Long-term care planning is about protecting assets, protecting the healthy spouse and preserving family choices.

The Family Question

If one spouse needs years of care, what happens to the healthy spouse's retirement?

“Won't Medicare pay for long-term care?”
Medicare can cover certain skilled-care services under limited conditions, but it is not designed to pay indefinitely for custodial long-term care.
“We have enough assets. Why buy coverage?”
Self-funding can be a valid strategy. The question is how much risk you want your retirement assets to absorb if one spouse experiences a prolonged care event.
“What if I pay premiums and never need care?”
Hybrid life and long-term-care products can address this concern. Depending on the policy, benefits may be available for qualified care while a death benefit may remain for beneficiaries if care benefits are not fully used.
“My kids will take care of me.”
They may be willing. A financial care plan gives your family the ability to choose how much caregiving they personally provide instead of making lack of money determine the answer.

04 · Tax-Free Inheritance

Make the transfer simple before your family has to.

Life insurance death benefits are generally received income-tax-free by beneficiaries under current federal tax law. Ownership, estate inclusion, trusts and state law require separate analysis.

The Legacy Question

When your family inherits, do they receive a pile of assets and decisions, or a coordinated plan with liquidity when they need it?

“My CPA handles my taxes. Isn't that enough?”
Your CPA is essential. Insurance design, beneficiary structure, estate documents, liquidity and retirement-income planning still need to work together. We help address the insurance and income-planning pieces and coordinate with your tax and legal professionals where appropriate.
“I asked AI. Why do I need a person?”
AI can explain concepts. It cannot determine your actual underwriting outcome, analyze an insurer's current illustration, understand every family tradeoff or execute the insurance application. Use AI to become better informed. Use licensed professionals to verify and implement.
“My wife knows everything about our finances.”
Then focus on continuity. Could she quickly locate every account, insurance policy, beneficiary designation, attorney, tax professional and source of retirement income during a crisis?
“My husband knows nothing about our finances.”
That is a planning risk. He does not need to become a financial expert. He needs a clear system showing where the assets are, who to call and what happens next.
“Can I leave an annuity to my children tax-free?”
Generally, gain in an inherited nonqualified annuity can be taxable as ordinary income when distributed to a non-spouse beneficiary. That is different from the general federal income-tax treatment of life insurance death benefits. We analyze annuity legacy benefits and life-insurance legacy strategies separately.
